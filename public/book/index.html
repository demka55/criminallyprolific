Steal One Hour

How founders who already won get their lives back without selling, quitting, or breaking the company

Nothing will break. That's the whole book.

This is not a productivity book.

You don't need more output per hour. You need to stop letting the founder role eat the rest of your life — without blowing up the thing you spent a decade building.

What it's not

Not a time-management system. Not a delegation framework. Not "work smarter." Not advice to sell, quit, or take a sabbatical.

What it is

A single provable dare — leave one hour early, tell no one, write what broke — repeated until you can prove to yourself that the company runs without you in it.

Who it's for

Founders 8–15 years in. You already won by most measures. The company works. You just can't stop working. Boris — 14 years in, 72-hour weeks, dreams of Paris. This is for him.

What you'll leave with

One real stolen hour per week. A written map of where the company actually depends on you. The first draft of your exit from founder creep.

The Steal One Hour Method

Ten steps. The first five build The Page — the map you'll use for the rest of your life.

The Dare — Before Chapter One

Leave one hour early tomorrow. Tell no one you're doing it. When you get home, write down everything that broke. Read that list. That list is the map. The book is about shrinking it to nothing.

Step 01

Find your values

Name the three things you'd refuse to give up. They become the filter for every decision in steps 2–10.

Step 02

Map yourself

Draw every role you play in the company. Not the org chart — the real one. Where does the company actually need you?

Step 03

5 Whys

Pick one role from your map. Ask why you're the one doing it, five times. The answer surprises everyone.

Step 04

Yes-and

For every "I can't step back because…" — yes, and what would have to be true for that to change?

Step 05

Pick your Tuesday

Choose the specific hour you're stealing first. Tuesday 4pm. Not "someday." The Page is now complete.

Step 06

Steal the next hour

Do the dare for real. Leave. Write the list. Compare it to The Page. The gap is your work for the next 90 days.

Step 07

Hand off, kill, or cage

Everything on the list gets one of three fates: someone else owns it, it doesn't happen, or it happens in a box you control.

Step 08

Slow reply

Respond to half your messages the next day instead of the same hour. Track what breaks. Almost nothing does.

Step 09

Sit in the empty hour

Do not fill the stolen hour with new work. The discomfort is the point — it's founder creep fighting back. Sit with it.

Step 10

Leave the engine running

Build the system that keeps the hour stolen: one weekly review, one person who can answer your question, one rule about where you are not.

Bonus

Sell the right way

If the stolen hours have taught you anything, it's that you've been building an acquirable company without knowing it. Here's how to find the buyer before you're ready to sell.

The Blueprint at a Glance

The core architecture of Steal One Hour — the promise, the reader, the method.

The Big Problem

Founder creep: the founder identity expands every year until it fills every hour you have. It doesn't feel like a trap. It feels like success. That's what makes it dangerous.

The Villain

"Freedom later" — the promise that keeps founders working 72-hour weeks indefinitely. When we hit $X. When we close this round. When we hire the VP. Later never arrives.

The Hero

Boris. Software company co-founder, 14 years in, 72-hour weeks, loves Paris and piano and tango and bonsai. He works every minute. He calls it dedication. The book calls it founder creep.

The Unit of Change

One hour. Not a lifestyle overhaul. Not a sabbatical. One specific hour, stolen from the company, written about, proven safe. Done once. Then again. Then it's just Tuesday.

The Method

The Dare + 10 steps + The Page. Five steps build your map. Five steps execute it. One bonus step for founders who realize the company is now worth selling.

The Heaven

Boris leaves at 4pm on a Tuesday. His phone doesn't ring. He goes to piano. The company is fine. He never knew it could be like this. This book knew it all along.

Central Promise

If you do The Dare — leave one hour early, tell no one, write what broke — and nothing breaks, you will have proven to yourself that the life you wanted when you started the company is available to you right now, without selling, without quitting, without breaking anything. The book is 300 pages of that proof.
